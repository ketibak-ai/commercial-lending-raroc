# Commercial Loan Pricing Policy

## Pricing Floors
Every new commercial loan or revolver must be priced using the RAROC pricing model. The standalone floor spread is the spread at which the facility alone earns the 12% hurdle. The relationship floor spread is the spread at which the entire relationship, including the new facility, earns the 12% hurdle. The recommended spread is the higher of the relationship floor and the cost floor.

## Cost Floor
No facility may be priced below the cost floor, defined as expected loss rate (PD x LGD) + liquidity premium (25 bps) + operating cost (45 bps). The cost floor applies even when relationship deposits and fees would otherwise justify a lower spread. Pricing below the cost floor is prohibited and cannot be approved by exception.

## Relationship Pricing Concessions
A concession below the standalone floor is permitted only when the relationship RAROC including the new deal remains at or above 12%. Concessions must be supported by existing deposit and treasury management revenue booked with the bank, not by projected or promised future business. Concessions greater than 50 bps require Regional Credit Officer approval.

## Pricing Exceptions and Approvals
Deals priced below the relationship floor are pricing exceptions. Exceptions up to 25 bps below floor may be approved by the Market President. Exceptions of 25 to 75 bps require Chief Credit Officer approval. Exceptions beyond 75 bps require the Pricing Committee. All exceptions must carry a remediation plan and are reviewed quarterly.

## Revolving Credit Facilities
Revolvers are priced on drawn spread plus an unused commitment fee. The undrawn portion consumes capital through the 75% credit conversion factor and contingent liquidity at 10 bps of undrawn commitments. Low-utilization revolvers frequently fall below the hurdle on a standalone basis and should be justified by the broader relationship.

## Repricing and Watch List
Any key relationship with RAROC below 15% is placed on the pricing watch list. Relationship managers must present a repricing or cross-sell plan within 60 days. Options include spread increases at renewal, higher unused fees, moving deposits into operating accounts, and adding treasury management services.
